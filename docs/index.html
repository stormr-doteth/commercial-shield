COMMERCIAL SHIELD

Hide TV commercials with one button.

Press a button on your remote when an ad comes on: the screen is covered and the sound goes off. Press it again when the game's back.

Install it free (5 minutes)

For Google TV, Android TV and Fire TV · No account · Open source

Why I made this

My kids kept seeing horror-movie trailers in the middle of baseball games. Ad blockers can't stop these, because streaming apps stitch the ads straight into the video. Paying for "no ads" doesn't remove them during live sports either.

So instead of trying to block the ads, Commercial Shield gives you a one-press way to cover them up.

What it does

🎯 One buttonYou pick any spare remote button, like an app shortcut you never use.
🙈 Works in every appMax, Peacock, YouTube TV, ESPN, Fox, Hulu Live, Sling, live TV…
🔇 Mutes the soundThen brings it back when you return.
⏱️ Break timerSo you can guess when the game's back. Usually about 2 minutes.
🔙 Can't get stuckBack closes it too, and it lifts itself after 5 minutes.
🔒 PrivateNo internet access, can't see your screen, nothing to sign up for.

Will it work on my TV?

It works on TVs and streaming sticks that run Google TV, Android TV or Fire TV. A quick way to tell: if your home screen says "Google TV" or your remote has a Google Assistant or Alexa button, you're probably good.

Install it (no computer needed)

Everything happens on your TV with the remote.

Your Downloader code
4540772

You'll type this in step 3.

  1. Get the free "Downloader" app

    On your TV, open the app store, search for Downloader (orange icon, by AFTVnews) and install it.

  2. Let Downloader install apps

    Google TV / Android TV: Settings → Apps → Security & restrictions → Unknown sources → turn on Downloader.

    Fire TV: Settings → My Fire TV → Developer options → Install unknown apps → turn on Downloader.

    Don't see Developer options on Fire TV? Go to Settings → My Fire TV → About and press OK on your device name 7 times, then go back.

  3. Type the code

    Open Downloader, select the address box, type 4540772 and press Go. When it finishes downloading, choose Install, then Open.

  4. Turn it on

    In Commercial Shield, choose Accessibility service. Your TV's settings open: find Commercial Shield, switch it On, then press Back.

    See a "Restricted setting" message? Go to Settings → Apps → Commercial Shield, choose Allow restricted settings (it may be under ⋮ or near the bottom), then try again.

  5. Pick your button

    Choose Shield button, then press the remote button you want to use. That's it.

    Good picks: an app button you never use (YouTube, Prime, Disney+…) or a color button. If your pick also opens its app, choose a different one.

Using it

  • Ad comes on: press your button. Screen covered, sound off.
  • Game's back: press it again, or press Back.
  • Forgot? It lifts itself after 5 minutes. You can change that in the app.

Questions

Is it safe? Why does it need "accessibility"?

Accessibility is the Android permission that lets an app notice remote button presses. Commercial Shield only listens for the one button you pick. It has no internet permission at all, so it can't send anything anywhere, and it's set up so it can't read your screen. All the code is public on GitHub, and every download is built and signed in public.

Does it block or skip ads?

No. The ad still plays underneath; your screen is just covered while it does. It's like pressing mute and throwing a blanket over the TV.

I can still hear the ad.

Some soundbars connected by HDMI ARC or optical control their own volume, so the TV's mute doesn't reach them. Use your soundbar's mute too.

Pressing my button does nothing.

Open Commercial Shield and check the service still says "On ✓". Some remote buttons are handled by the TV's hardware and Android never sees them. If the app doesn't react when you pick the button, try a different one.

How do I remove it?

Settings → Apps → Commercial Shield → Uninstall. Nothing is left behind.

Can it detect commercials automatically?

Not reliably. Streaming apps protect their video so other apps can't see it, and the ads are stitched right into the stream. One button you control is the honest version that works everywhere.
